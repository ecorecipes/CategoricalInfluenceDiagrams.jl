CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams.jl

Open influence diagrams as structured cospans, following ADR 0018 of the ecosystem. The package is the decision-layer analogue of CategoricalBayesianNetworks.jl: it gives the influence diagrams of InfluenceDiagrams.jl open boundaries and composes them, keeping Catlab out of the model layer (ADR 0009).

What is here

  • Open influence diagrams. An OpenInfluenceDiagram is a Catlab structured cospan whose apex is an InfluenceDiagram and whose feet are VariableSpaces, the same feet as those of an open Bayesian network. Build one with Open.

  • Observability. Every interface variable is :observed, remembered information that a later decision may read, or :hidden. The flags sit beside the cospan, one per interface position, rather than in the feet, so that the feet stay interchangeable with those of CategoricalBayesianNetworks.jl. Everything is hidden unless declared observed. Two interfaces that agree on variables and states but not on flags do not match (ObservabilityMismatchError).

  • The typed-interface rule, checked by validation_errors and on construction:

    1. every variable has at most one generator, a chance mechanism or a decision;
    2. an input-foot variable has no generator in the apex;
    3. a decision reads only internal variables and observed input-foot variables;
    4. a utility scope may read any apex variable, inputs included;
    5. the causal-plus-information graph is acyclic;
    6. composition preserves no-forgetting when the components have it: checked on every composite, reported as a NoForgettingError naming the decision and the missing information arc.

  • Composition. compose, glue, otimes and oapply are pushouts or coproducts of ACSets, with the rule checked again on the composite. Decisions and utilities stay inside their components; only variables cross a boundary.

  • Open Bayesian networks embed with every interface variable hidden: OpenInfluenceDiagram(o::OpenBayesNet), inverted by OpenBayesNet(d).

  • Semantics for a fixed strategy. A RewardKernel (K, W) pairs a FiniteKernel K from the input interface to the output interface with a table W of the same shape: the probability-weighted reward earned on the way. Reward kernels compose by (K₁, W₁) ; (K₂, W₂) = (K₁K₂, W₁K₂ + K₁W₂) and tensor by (K₁ ⊗ K₂, W₁ ⊗ K₂ + K₁ ⊗ W₂), with identities, copies, discards and swaps lifted from FiniteKernels.jl with zero reward (compose, otimes, identity_reward_kernel, copy_reward_kernel, discard_reward_kernel, swap_reward_kernel). Discard is not natural: dropping an output keeps the reward earned on the way to it.

  • Interpretation. interpret maps an open influence diagram and a deterministic strategy to its reward kernel, in the layout CategoricalBayesianNetworks.jl uses for open networks. The strategy of a composite is the disjoint union of its components' strategies (merge_strategies). On a closed composite the result is (1, EU), with EU the expected utility that InfluenceDiagrams.jl computes, and the test suite checks interpret against that oracle under compose, glue, otimes and oapply, on every strategy of the umbrella and oil-wildcatter splits, on the SPEC §46 grazing diagram at two boundaries, and on random small diagrams.

  • Optimisation, contextual and never isolated. A composite is an ordinary InfluenceDiagram, and optimize hands a closed composite to the solvers of InfluenceDiagrams.jl. An open component f : X → Y has no expected utility of its own: contextual_value scores it under a fixed strategy against an explicit prior μ on its joint input states and an explicit continuation c on its joint output states, Σ_x μ(x) Σ_y (W(y, x) + K(y | x) c(y)), and contextual_optimize maximises that over the legal deterministic strategies. For a serial composite f ⋅ g the contexts are the suffix's value function (continuation_value) and the prefix's boundary law (boundary_law), and the contextual identity EU_{f;g}(σ, τ; μ, c) = EU_f(σ; μ, c_τ) = L_σ + EU_g(τ; ν_σ, c) holds; the test suite checks it against the closed-composite oracle, and checks that both orders of contextual maximisation reach the optimum the solvers find. There is deliberately no "optimise each component, then compose" operation. The test suite reproduces the counterexample of the research bundle's Lean track: a fair hidden bit, an early action with utility -[A], a late action that observes only A with utilities 4[A] and 2[B = H]. Each component optimised in isolation (zero continuation, actual induced prior) composes to the value 1, while the composite attains 4. Maximising separately at each hidden boundary state would need a policy that reads the hidden input, which rule 3 rejects.

Not yet here

ADR 0018 also plans the Lean proofs of the semantics and of the optimisation results, and vignettes. They are later milestones. The functoriality of interpret, the contextual identity and the counterexample are checked numerically, not proved. Randomised (behavioural) policies and evidence across a boundary are outside the first release. The Lean project in proofs/ builds on the proof projects of CategoricalBayesianNetworks.jl and InfluenceDiagrams.jl and so far states only boundary observability and information locality.

Example

julia> chance = Open(influence_diagram(:Weather => [:sunny, :rainy],
                                       :Forecast => [:sunny, :cloudy, :rainy];
                                       mechanisms = [:Weather => (), :Forecast => :Weather],
                                       closed = false);
                     outputs = [:Weather, :Forecast], observed = [:Forecast]);

julia> decide = Open(influence_diagram(:Weather => [:sunny, :rainy],
                                       :Forecast => [:sunny, :cloudy, :rainy],
                                       :Umbrella => [:take, :leave];
                                       decisions = [:Umbrella => :Forecast],
                                       utilities = [:U => (:Weather, :Umbrella)],
                                       closed = false);
                     inputs = [:Weather, :Forecast], observed = [:Forecast]);

julia> is_isomorphic(apex(compose(chance, decide)), umbrella_diagram())
true

With the numbers of umbrella_model, the chance component's reward kernel composed with the decision component's equals the composite's, whose reward is the expected utility:

julia> m = umbrella_model();

julia> ks = Dict(x => kernel(m, x) for x in (:Weather, :Forecast));

julia> σ = Strategy(:Umbrella => deterministic_policy(m, :Umbrella, [:leave, :leave, :take]));

julia> r = compose(interpret(chance, ks, bound_utilities(m), Strategy()),
                   interpret(decide, ks, bound_utilities(m), σ));

julia> r ≈ interpret(compose(chance, decide), ks, bound_utilities(m), σ)
true

julia> reward(r)[] ≈ expected_utility(m, σ) ≈ 77
true

The closed composite is solved by InfluenceDiagrams.jl's solvers; the open decision component reaches the same optimum when it is optimised against the law the chance component induces on the boundary:

julia> optimize(compose(chance, decide), ks, bound_utilities(m)).expected_utility ≈ 77
true

julia> ν = boundary_law(chance, ks, bound_utilities(m), Strategy());

julia> contextual_optimize(decide, ks, bound_utilities(m); prior = ν).expected_utility ≈ 77
true
