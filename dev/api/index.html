API Reference

Every exported name of CategoricalBayesianNetworks.jl and InfluenceDiagrams.jl is re-exported, among them the Catlab names those packages re-export (apex, legs, feet, dom, codom, compose, otimes, munit, id, oapply, @relation) and every exception type of the lower packages, as the same bindings. Those names are documented by their packages. This page documents the names this package defines, and the methods it adds to the re-exported functions: among them compose, otimes, dom, codom, isapprox and kernel on a RewardKernel, interpret on an open influence diagram, and optimize on a closed composite.

CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams.CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams — Module
CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams

Open influence diagrams as structured cospans (ADR 0018). This is the decision-layer analogue of CategoricalBayesianNetworks.jl: it adds to InfluenceDiagrams.jl, which stays Catlab-free (ADR 0009), the open-system machinery of the categorical layer.

  • Open diagrams (src/open.jl): OpenInfluenceDiagram, a Catlab structured cospan with an InfluenceDiagram apex and VariableSpace feet, the feet being the same type as those of an open Bayesian network. Every interface variable is also declared :observed (remembered information a later decision may read) or :hidden. The typed-interface rule of open Bayesian networks is extended to decisions and utilities and checked by validation_errors. An OpenBayesNet embeds with every interface variable :hidden.
  • Composition (src/composition.jl): compose, glue, otimes and oapply, each a pushout or coproduct of ACSets, with the rule (including its no-forgetting clause) checked again on the composite.
  • Semantics for a fixed strategy (src/semantics.jl): RewardKernel, the expectation-semiring kernel category over FiniteKernels.jl, and interpret, which maps an open influence diagram and a deterministic strategy to its reward kernel, functorially for compose and otimes.
  • Optimisation, contextual and never isolated (src/optimization.jl): optimize on a closed composite, and contextual_value and contextual_optimize on an open component against an explicit boundary prior and continuation value, with continuation_value and boundary_law the contexts a serial composite induces. With evidence (ADR 0021), boundary_state and continuation_effect carry the evidence mass across a boundary, and conditional_value is the expected utility given the evidence.
  • Reward certificates (src/certificates.jl, ADR 0019): export_reward_certificate writes a diagram, a strategy and the reward kernel interpret computed for it as an OpenInfluence.RewardCertificate/v1 record, which the Lean checker in proofs/ reads.

Every exported name of CategoricalBayesianNetworks and InfluenceDiagrams is re-exported, so using CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams is a superset of using both.

Part of the ecorecipes compositional Bayesian-network ecosystem.

CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams.DiagramInterfaceError — Type
DiagramInterfaceError(rule, variable, by, kind)

A violation of the typed-interface rule of open influence diagrams (ADR 0018, decision 2) that BayesianNetworks and InfluenceDiagrams have no type for:

  • rule 2, an input-foot variable has a generator in the apex: input variable is generated by the mechanism (kind = :mechanism) or the decision (kind = :decision) named by;
  • rule 3, a decision reads a hidden input: decision by has variable, an input-foot variable declared :hidden, in its information set (kind = :hidden_input).

The other rules are reported by the lower packages' types: rule 1 by DuplicateGeneratorError and DecisionUniquenessError, rule 4 (a utility scope reads an apex variable) by DanglingReferenceError, rule 5 by CyclicBayesNetError, InvalidInformationSetError and DecisionPrecedenceCycleError, and rule 6 by NoForgettingError.

CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams.NoForgettingError — Type
NoForgettingError(decision, variable, earlier)

Rule 6 of the typed-interface rule (ADR 0018, decision 2): the components of a composite had no-forgetting, but the composite does not. Decision earlier precedes decision in the composite, and variable (in the information set of earlier, or its action) is not in the information set of decision: the arc variable -> decision is missing. Across a boundary this means that variable was not passed on as an :observed interface variable, or that the later component's decision does not read it.

ObservabilityMismatchError(variable, position, left, right, context)

Two interface variables that are glued together agree in name, states and reference but not in observability: one side declares variable :observed (remembered information a later decision may read) and the other :hidden (ADR 0018, decision 2). position is the index in the interface (or in the along list, or the junction) where the difference was found, left and right are the two flags, and context says which operation compared them.

CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams.OpenInfluenceDiagram — Type
OpenInfluenceDiagram

An open influence diagram (ADR 0018, decision 2): a Catlab structured cospan (InfluenceDiagramCospan) whose apex is an InfluenceDiagram and whose two feet are VariableSpaces, together with an OBSERVABILITY flag for every variable of each foot. Build one with Open, or embed an OpenBayesNet with OpenInfluenceDiagram(o::OpenBayesNet); combine with compose, glue, otimes and oapply. Inspect with apex, legs, feet, dom, codom, inputs, outputs, input_space, output_space, input_variables, output_variables, left_leg, right_leg, input_observability, output_observability, observed_inputs, observed_outputs and structured_cospan.

Only variables cross the boundary: decisions and utilities stay inside their component.

The inner constructor OpenInfluenceDiagram(cospan, input_observability, output_observability) checks only that there is one flag per foot variable and that each is :observed or :hidden (ArgumentError otherwise); it does not check the typed-interface rule, which validate does.

CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams.RewardCertificateError — Type
RewardCertificateError(code, path, message)

export_reward_certificate cannot write an OpenInfluence.RewardCertificate/v1 record (ADR 0019) for the diagram and tables it was given. code names the condition, path locates it in the certificate (dotted keys with 0-based array indices, such as network.mechanisms[1].kernelRef, or the companion key for exact_tables) and message names the parts and variables involved. The codes are:

  • :unset_reference: a space_ref or kernel_ref has no value; the certificate records every reference (NoRef() records none);
  • :unsupported_reference: a reference of a type the format has no tag for (a user-defined KernelRef subtype);
  • :policy_reference_on_chance: a chance mechanism carries a PolicyRef, which the format reserves for decisions;
  • :inconsistent_decision: two equally named decisions, which share one policy, differ in the number of their information inputs, in those inputs' state counts or in their action's state count;
  • :too_many_entries: the certificate would hold more than max_entries cells;
  • :non_finite: a cell to be written is not finite;
  • :missing_companion, :extra_companion and :companion_rounding: in rational_exact mode, a chance or utility cell has no exact companion, a companion matches no cell, or a companion does not round (to nearest, ties to even) to the Float64 cell it accompanies.

Invalid keyword values raise ArgumentError instead, and structural and semantic failures keep the exceptions of validate and interpret.

CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams.RewardKernel — Type
RewardKernel(K::FiniteKernel, W::AbstractArray{<:Real})
RewardKernel(K::FiniteKernel)

A morphism of the expectation-semiring kernel category (ADR 0018, decision 3): the semantics of an open influence diagram X → Y under a fixed strategy.

  • K is a FiniteKernel X → Y: K(y | x) is the probability of reaching the output state y from the input state x.
  • W is a real table of exactly the shape of K's table, outputs first: W[y..., x...] is the expectation, given x, of the reward earned on the way times the indicator that the output state is y. It may be signed. Summed over the outputs it is the expected reward given x.

Without W the reward is zero: this lifts a kernel such as an identity, a copy, a discard or a swap into the category. A W of another shape, or with an entry that is not finite, raises ArgumentError.

Read the parts with kernel(r) and reward, the spaces with dom(r) and codom(r). Combine with compose and otimes; the structure maps are identity_reward_kernel, copy_reward_kernel, discard_reward_kernel and swap_reward_kernel. Joint states are ordered as FiniteKernels' joint_states (the first variable varies fastest), so reward_matrix and kernel_matrix(kernel(r)) share their rows and columns.

AlgebraicInterfaces.compose — Method
compose(A::OpenInfluenceDiagram, B::OpenInfluenceDiagram; no_forgetting = true) -> OpenInfluenceDiagram
A ⋅ B

Strict sequential composition. The output interface of A must equal the input interface of B position by position: names, states with positions and references (BayesianNetworks' InterfaceMismatchError names the first difference), then observability (ObservabilityMismatchError). Both diagrams are validated first. The result is a Catlab pushout of the two apexes along the shared foot, with the inputs of A and the outputs of B and their flags.

The composite is validated (see validation_errors; with unique names when both pieces had them), then rule 6: when both components have no-forgetting, so must the composite, or a NoForgettingError names the first decision that forgets and the missing information arc. A decision of B that comes after a decision of A has to know what that decision knew and did, and it can only learn it through observed interface variables. Here, decision D_k precedes D_l when a directed path of causal, information or precedence arcs runs from the action of D_k to the action of D_l, and no-forgetting asks that the information set of D_l contain that of D_k and its action. For decisions that such paths order totally this is InfluenceDiagrams' is_no_forgetting. With no_forgetting = false rule 6 is skipped, and the result may be a limited-memory diagram, which ExhaustivePolicySearch solves on the closed composite.

Unset references, utility_ref included, survive the pushout unchanged.

AlgebraicInterfaces.compose — Method
compose(a::RewardKernel, b::RewardKernel, cs::RewardKernel...) -> RewardKernel
a ⋅ b

Sequential composition in diagrammatic order (first a, then b): (K₁, W₁) ; (K₂, W₂) = (K₁K₂, W₁K₂ + K₁W₂), where K₁K₂ is FiniteKernels' compose_kernel. The reward earned in a is carried through b's kernel and the reward earned in b is weighted by the probability of reaching each of its inputs. Further reward kernels are folded on from the left. codom(a) must equal dom(b) (FiniteKernels' SpaceMismatchError).

BayesianNetworks.validation_errors — Method
validation_errors(o::OpenInfluenceDiagram; unique_names = false) -> Vector{Exception}

All problems of the open influence diagram o under the typed-interface rule of ADR 0018 (decision 2), without stopping at the first. The structural checks of the apex come first (InfluenceDiagrams' validation_errors, always with closed = false, since an input has no generator); they cover rules 1, 4 and 5:

  1. every variable has at most one generator, a chance mechanism or a decision (DuplicateGeneratorError, DecisionUniquenessError);
  2. a utility scope may read any apex variable, inputs included; it may not read a variable that is not in the apex (DanglingReferenceError);
  3. the causal-plus-information graph is acyclic (CyclicBayesNetError, InvalidInformationSetError with reason :downstream, DecisionPrecedenceCycleError).

Then the interface checks, which are skipped when the apex has a DanglingReferenceError or a MissingAttributeError. The legs must be natural and preserve names, references and states (InterfaceError 5), and the input leg injective (InterfaceError 2), as for an open Bayesian network. Then

  1. an input-foot variable has no generator in the apex (DiagramInterfaceError with rule 2);
    • and every variable without a generator is an input (InterfaceError 3, as for an open network: an action variable has a generator, its decision);
  2. a decision's information inputs are internal variables or observed input-foot variables (DiagramInterfaceError with rule 3).

Rule 6, that composition preserves no-forgetting, concerns a composite and its components, not one diagram, and is checked by the operations that build composites (see compose). An output variable may be declared observed or hidden freely: whether a later decision may read it is decided by the flag, not by where it came from.

CategoricalBayesianNetworks.Open — Method
Open(id::InfluenceDiagram; inputs = Symbol[], outputs = Symbol[], observed = Symbol[],
     validate = true) -> OpenInfluenceDiagram
Open(id::InfluenceDiagram, inputs, outputs; observed = Symbol[], validate = true)

Make id an open influence diagram with the named variables as input and output interfaces, in the given order. Each foot is a VariableSpace copying the variables' names, references and states (with positions), exactly as for an OpenBayesNet. Names may repeat in outputs (an implicit copy) but not in inputs.

observed names the interface variables that are :observed: remembered information that a decision may read. Every other interface variable is :hidden. It takes two forms.

  • A vector of names. A name in it is observed on every foot it appears on, and a name that is on neither foot raises ArgumentError.
  • A named tuple (inputs = [...], outputs = [...]), either field optional (default empty), which flags each foot separately: the names in observed.inputs are observed on the input foot, those in observed.outputs on the output foot. A name in observed.inputs that is not an input, or in observed.outputs that is not an output, raises ArgumentError naming them, as does any other field or an entry that is not a Symbol. The vector [n...] is the named tuple that lists each n on every foot it is on.

The default is that everything is hidden. It is the conservative choice: a decision may never read a hidden input (rule 3), so an interface carries information only where it says so, and an OpenBayesNet embeds with every variable hidden.

Per-foot flags matter for a pass-through variable, one on both feet:

  • observed on the input foot, hidden on the output foot: the boundary forgets the variable. The component's decisions may read it, but a decision downstream cannot, since the next component must take it hidden to match (or ObservabilityMismatchError) and rule 3 then forbids reading it. If such a decision comes after one that read the variable, the composite fails rule 6 (NoForgettingError).
  • hidden on the input foot, observed on the output foot: the component reveals the variable, as a perfect observation made inside the component, and a downstream decision may read it. The component's own decisions still may not, since rule 3 is about inputs. Composites are validated again, so a decision downstream still cannot read the variable when a composite's input foot hides it: compose(R, B), with R revealing X to a decision of B that reads it, fails rule 3 on the composite, because X is a hidden input of R ⋅ B; composed after a component that generates X, it is internal and legal.

With validate = true the typed-interface rule is checked (see validate) and the first violation thrown. An unknown name raises BayesianNetworks' UnknownVariableError.

Example

julia> id = influence_diagram(:Weather => [:sunny, :rainy],
                              :Forecast => [:sunny, :cloudy, :rainy],
                              :Umbrella => [:take, :leave];
                              decisions = [:Umbrella => :Forecast],
                              utilities = [:U => (:Weather, :Umbrella)], closed = false);

julia> B = Open(id; inputs = [:Weather, :Forecast], observed = [:Forecast]);

julia> inputs(B), input_observability(B)
([:Weather, :Forecast], [:hidden, :observed])

julia> R = Open(influence_diagram(:X => [:a, :b]; closed = false);
                inputs = [:X], outputs = [:X], observed = (outputs = [:X],));

julia> input_observability(R), output_observability(R)
([:hidden], [:observed])

CategoricalBayesianNetworks.glue — Method
glue(A::OpenInfluenceDiagram, B::OpenInfluenceDiagram; along, no_forgetting = true) -> OpenInfluenceDiagram

Glue selected outputs of A to selected inputs of B: each a => b in along::Vector{Pair{Symbol,Symbol}} names an output variable of A and an input variable of B, which must carry the same states (positions included), reference and observability, but may differ in name (the glued variable keeps its name in A). BayesianNetworks' InterfaceMismatchError reports a name that is not in the relevant interface, a variable glued twice, or the first pair whose states or references differ, and ObservabilityMismatchError a pair whose flags differ. A rename whose new name is already taken in apex(B) is refused with BayesianNetworks' NameClashError.

The result has inputs inputs(A) followed by the unglued inputs of B, and outputs the unglued outputs of A followed by outputs(B), each with its flag. It is validated as a composite, rule 6 included, exactly as for compose. Both operands are validated first, as compose validates them. Unlike the open-network glue this matters: rule 3 concerns a component's own input foot, and gluing turns a glued input into an internal variable, so a decision that reads a hidden input of B would otherwise pass on the composite.

CategoricalBayesianNetworks.interpret — Method
interpret(o::OpenInfluenceDiagram, kernels, utilities, σ::Strategy; evidence = nothing,
          max_states = 1_000_000, atol = DEFAULT_ATOL) -> RewardKernel
interpret(o::OpenInfluenceDiagram, m::InfluenceDiagramModel, σ::Strategy = strategy(m); kwargs...)

The semantics ⟦o⟧_σ : ⊗ inputs → ⊗ outputs of an open influence diagram under the fixed deterministic strategy σ (ADR 0018, decision 3), as a RewardKernel (K, W). For each joint input state x, K(y | x) is the probability that the outputs take the joint state y, and W[y..., x...] the expectation of the total (additive) utility of the apex times the indicator that the outputs are y. The inputs have no mechanism, so the apex is conditioned on them: the product of the chance kernels and the policy kernels is summed over every apex variable that is not an output, with the inputs held at x.

The layout is that of CategoricalBayesianNetworks' interpret for an open network: the input and output spaces have one axis per interface variable, in interface order (an output listed twice is copied, an input that is also an output passes through), and the joint states are enumerated with the first variable fastest. On a closed composite (no inputs, no outputs) the result is the 1 × 1 reward kernel with K = 1 and W the expected utility that InfluenceDiagrams' expected_utility computes on the instantiated diagram.

evidence (ADR 0021) is evidence on apex variables: a pair, a vector of pairs or a dictionary, each pair a variable name with either an observed state, :X => :x (hard evidence), or a likelihood vector, :X => [λ₁, λ₂, …] (soft or virtual evidence), one entry in [0, 1] per state of X, the probability of the observation given each state. Each observed variable contributes the indicator of its observed state or its likelihood, inputs and outputs included, so K(y | x) becomes the probability of reaching y and seeing the evidence, and W the reward earned on those paths. An indicator likelihood is hard evidence. K is then substochastic: it is built unchecked, and its column sums are the probability of the evidence given each input state. Nothing is normalised, since the evidence mass must cross the boundary to the rest of the problem (normalising each component on its own evidence is not sound; see conditional_value). Reward kernels with evidence compose as any others: interpret(compose(A, B), merge_strategies(σ, τ); evidence = e) is compose(interpret(A, σ; evidence = e_A), interpret(B, τ; evidence = e_B)), where e_A and e_B are the parts of e on each apex; evidence on a variable of the shared boundary may be given to either or both, since an indicator is idempotent. An unknown variable raises UnknownVariableError, an unknown state UnknownStateError, evidence on a decision's action EvidenceOnActionError (an action is chosen, not observed), and two different observations of one variable, or a likelihood of the wrong length or with an entry outside [0, 1], ArgumentError. The evidence errors of ADR 0014 (ImpossibleEvidenceError, IndeterminatePosteriorError) record the hard evidence only, since their field holds states.

The tables come from the apex's references, as instantiate reads them:

  • kernels is a dictionary keyed by KernelRef or by target variable name, exactly as for CategoricalBayesianNetworks' interpret (MissingKernelError, KernelBindingError);
  • utilities a dictionary of AbstractUtilitys keyed by utility name (as bound_utilities of a model) or by utility_ref (MissingUtilityError when one does not resolve, UtilityScopeError when one does not fit its scope);
  • a model m supplies kernels(m) and bound_utilities(m), and σ defaults to its strategy. Its evidence is used as the evidence, together with the evidence keyword (a variable given different states by the two raises ArgumentError).

σ needs a DeterministicPolicy or ConstantPolicy for every decision name of the apex (IncompleteStrategyError), which may be a DeterministicPolicy, a ConstantPolicy or a StochasticPolicy (a behavioural policy, ADR 0022: a decision then draws its action from the policy's table given its information, independently at every use), checked against the decision's information and action axes (PolicySignatureError); policies for other decisions are ignored, as instantiate ignores them, and decisions that share a name share their policy. A decision that reads an observed input reads that input's state directly: the policy is evaluated at the input state x, which is what rule 3 makes legal. Build the strategy of a composite with merge_strategies.

o is validated first. At most max_states joint states of the apex are enumerated (ModelTooLargeError); atol is the normalisation tolerance of the kernels.

Functoriality, checked numerically by the test suite against the closed-composite oracle: interpret(compose(A, B), merge_strategies(σ, τ)) ≈ compose(interpret(A, σ), interpret(B, τ)), and likewise for otimes, with and without evidence.

CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams.best_response_dynamics — Method
best_response_dynamics(components, kernels, utilities, σ::Strategy; prior = nothing,
                       continuation = nothing, evidence = nothing,
                       no_forgetting = true, tol = 1e-9, max_rounds = 100,
                       max_policies = 1_000_000, max_states, atol) -> NamedTuple

Starting from the strategy σ of the whole chain, visit the components in order, replacing a component's part of the strategy by a best response in its current context (contextual_optimize) whenever that raises the component's value by more than tol, and repeat until a full round changes nothing. The result is an equilibrium (is_equilibrium), and the value of the whole chain never decreases, so the procedure ends; max_rounds bounds the rounds (converged = false when it is reached).

It returns (strategy, value, rounds, converged, values, changed): the final strategy, the chain's value under it (contextual_value of the composite in the chain's context), the number of rounds, whether the last round changed nothing, the chain's value before the first and after every change, and the index of the component changed at each change.

This is single policy updating at the level of components (Lauritzen and Nilsson's local optimisation for limited-memory influence diagrams). It finds an equilibrium, which can be worse than the optimum; which one depends on σ and on the order of the components. To find the optimum, use optimize on the closed composite.

Given evidence (evidence, or a chain context that carries it), every value is the conditional value of the whole chain given all of the evidence (conditional_value), which each best response maximises and which therefore never decreases.

CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams.boundary_law — Method
boundary_law(r::RewardKernel; prior = nothing, atol = DEFAULT_ATOL) -> Vector{Float64}
boundary_law(o::OpenInfluenceDiagram, kernels, utilities, σ::Strategy; prior, kwargs...)
boundary_law(o::OpenInfluenceDiagram, m::InfluenceDiagramModel, σ = strategy(m); kwargs...)

The law ν_σ = μ K_f that a prefix f : X → Y under the fixed strategy σ induces on its joint output states, in joint_states order: ν_σ(y) = Σ_x μ(x) K_f(y | x), with the prior μ given as in contextual_value (left out when f has no inputs). It is the joint law of the hidden state and the remembered information at the boundary, not a product of marginals. With evidence in f (or a state prior carrying evidence) the law is unnormalised: its mass is the probability of the evidence so far, and it is not a prior for the suffix. Pass boundary_state instead, or divide by the mass for the posterior law of the boundary given that evidence (ADR 0021).

CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams.component_contexts — Method
component_contexts(components, kernels, utilities, σ::Strategy; prior = nothing,
                   continuation = nothing, evidence = nothing, no_forgetting = true,
                   max_states, atol) -> Vector{NamedTuple}

The context (prior = μᵢ, continuation = cᵢ) of each component of the chain components[1] ⋅ components[2] ⋅ … ⋅ components[n] under the strategy σ of the whole chain (ADR 0020). prior and continuation are the chain's own context, given as for contextual_value on the composite; they may be left out for an empty interface.

Then conditional_value(components[i], …, σ; prior = μᵢ, continuation = cᵢ, evidence = eᵢ) is exactly the whole chain's expected utility given all of the evidence, for every i. CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams.conditional_value — Method
conditional_value(r::RewardKernel; prior = nothing, continuation = nothing, atol = DEFAULT_ATOL) -> Float64
conditional_value(o::OpenInfluenceDiagram, kernels, utilities, σ::Strategy; prior, continuation, evidence, max_states, atol)
conditional_value(o::OpenInfluenceDiagram, m::InfluenceDiagramModel, σ = strategy(m); kwargs...)

The expected utility given the evidence (ADR 0021) of an open component f : X → Y under the fixed strategy σ, in the context of prior and continuation given as in contextual_value: the evidence-weighted value divided by the evidence_mass,

E[U | evidence] = Σ_x (ρ(x) m'(x) + ν(x) c'(x)) / Σ_x ν(x) m'(x),

with m' = Kᵀ m and c' = Wᵀ m + Kᵀ c. The evidence is that of f (the evidence keyword of the diagram forms, checked as for interpret), of a state prior (boundary_state) and of an effect continuation (continuation_effect). Since the numerator and the mass are both pairings that are the same at every cut of a chain, the conditional value of a composite is computed at any boundary from the two contexts there; on a closed composite it is InfluenceDiagrams' expected_utility of the model with the evidence observed. Normalising each component on its own evidence before composing is not sound (the Lean module Evidence, local_normalisation_obstruction): the evidence mass has to cross the boundary. Without evidence the result is contextual_value.

Evidence mass follows ADR 0014. A binary64 run is trusted when its mass is finite and at least floatmin and none of its products of nonzero operands fell below floatmin (the reward-kernel form checks this conservatively, from the smallest nonzero entries of the state, the kernel and the effect). Otherwise the value is recomputed exactly in Rational{BigInt} and rounded correctly: the diagram forms recompute the reward kernel from the tables, so a mass that only underflowed is resolved, and the reward-kernel form, which has only its tables, recomputes the pairing from them. A mass that is exactly zero raises ImpossibleEvidenceError. When f's tables hold tolerated negative entries (in [-atol, 0)) and there is evidence, the diagram forms raise IndeterminatePosteriorError if the mass is not larger than the budget (1 + atol)^n - 1, n the number of variables with a mechanism or a policy, or if the exact fallback would be needed; a mass that comes out negative raises it in every form.

The diagram forms take interpret's arguments and errors; the model form also uses the model's evidence.

CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams.contextual_optimize — Method
contextual_optimize(o::OpenInfluenceDiagram, kernels, utilities; prior = nothing,
                    continuation = nothing, evidence = nothing,
                    max_policies = 1_000_000, brute_force = false, max_states,
                    atol) -> DecisionSolution
contextual_optimize(o::OpenInfluenceDiagram, m::InfluenceDiagramModel; kwargs...)

Maximise contextual_value over the deterministic strategies of the decisions of the open component o : X → Y, in the explicit context of a prior μ on its joint input states and a continuation c on its joint output states, given as for contextual_value (ADR 0018, decision 4). The result is InfluenceDiagrams' DecisionSolution: the best value EU_o(σ*; μ, c), a best strategy σ* with a DeterministicPolicy for every decision name of o, and diagnostics that include nstrategies and method.

Only legal strategies are searched: each policy is a function of its decision's declared information set, which by rule 3 holds internal and observed input variables only. A hidden input is never read, so no strategy can maximise separately at each hidden boundary state; the bundle shows that doing so changes the problem (INF-HIDDEN-OBSTRUCTION).

By default the search is InfluenceDiagrams' own: the apex of o is bound to the tables of kernels and utilities as an InfluenceDiagramModel whose input variables keep no mechanism (they are its exogenous variables), the context becomes a DecisionContext with μ as the prior over the input variables and c as the terminal utility over the output variables (a repeated output repeats its name and is read on the diagonal; a pass-through variable is both), and optimize(m, ExhaustivePolicySearch(; max_policies); context, max_states, atol) solves it. The result and its diagnostics are that function's ((nstrategies, method = :value_table), with exact_fallback = true when its value table falls back to scoring strategy by strategy). It enumerates the strategies in the order of all_deterministic_strategies(apex(o)) (all_deterministic_policies for each decision, in decision_order, the first decision fastest) and returns the first one attaining the maximum. More than max_policies strategies raise PolicySearchTooLargeError. Decision variable elimination is never used, since it takes no context.

With brute_force = true every strategy, in the same order, is scored by contextual_value through interpret instead, an independent path that is slower and is the oracle the test suite compares with; its diagnostics are (nstrategies, method = :brute_force).

With evidence (ADR 0021), in o (the evidence keyword, checked as for interpret) or carried by the context (a state prior from boundary_state, an effect continuation from continuation_effect), the objective is the conditional_value, the expected utility given all of the evidence, as InfluenceDiagrams' ExhaustivePolicySearch maximises it on a closed model with evidence. By default one pass over the apex's joint states, with the decisions left free, builds value tables: for each joint state of the decisions' information and action variables, the evidence mass and the evidence-weighted value a strategy selecting it contributes, the state and the effect folded in as factors on the inputs and outputs. Each strategy, in the same order, is then scored by summing the cells it selects (method = :conditional_value_table). A strategy whose summed mass is below binary64's normal range is scored by conditional_value instead, with its exact fallback (ADR 0014), and if the pass computed a product of nonzero operands below floatmin, or met a tolerated negative entry with evidence, every strategy is (exact_fallback = true). With brute_force = true every strategy is scored by conditional_value through interpret (method = :conditional), the independent path the tests compare with. Strategies under which the evidence has probability exactly zero are skipped, and if every strategy is, ImpossibleEvidenceError is raised. When the evidence lies downstream of a decision, its probability depends on the strategy, and the conditional value is not the evidence-weighted value up to a constant. A state prior with zero reward and mass one within atol, or an effect continuation whose probability row is one within atol, counts as a plain prior or continuation.

The context is what makes the result meaningful. With continuation the continuation_value of a suffix g under τ, the optimum is the best prefix strategy against that suffix; maximising that over τ gives the optimum of o ⋅ g, and so does maximising L_σ + contextual_optimize(g; prior = boundary_law(o, σ)) over σ. A component optimised against a context other than the one its neighbours induce (a zero continuation, say) can choose a strategy that composes badly: the bundle's Lean witness composes isolated optima to 1 where the composite attains 4 (INF-ISOLATED-OBSTRUCTION). There is no operation that optimises components one by one and composes them.

Arguments are checked as for interpret and contextual_value: o is validated, the prior and continuation are checked as there (a prior's entries nonnegative and summing to one within atol, as DecisionContext requires), at most max_states joint states of the apex are enumerated (ModelTooLargeError), and missing tables raise MissingKernelError or MissingUtilityError. The apex's variable, decision and utility names must be unique, since the search keys policies and tables by name: equally named decisions (a diagram tensored with itself, say) raise ArgumentError on both paths, while interpret and contextual_value still accept them. The model form reads kernels(m), bound_utilities(m) and the model's evidence, joined with the keyword's.

CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams.contextual_value — Method
contextual_value(r::RewardKernel; prior = nothing, continuation = nothing, atol = DEFAULT_ATOL) -> Float64
contextual_value(o::OpenInfluenceDiagram, kernels, utilities, σ::Strategy; prior, continuation, evidence, kwargs...)
contextual_value(o::OpenInfluenceDiagram, m::InfluenceDiagramModel, σ = strategy(m); kwargs...)

The expected utility EU_f(σ; μ, c) of an open component f : X → Y under the fixed strategy σ, in the context of a prior μ on its inputs and a continuation c on its outputs (ADR 0018, decision 4; the bundle's Score):

EU_f(σ; μ, c) = Σ_x μ(x) Σ_y (W(y, x) + K(y | x) c(y)),

where (K, W) is the reward kernel of f under σ. The diagram forms compute it with interpret, whose arguments and keywords (evidence, max_states, atol) they take.

  • prior is a real vector over the joint input states, in joint_states order of dom(r) (the first input varies fastest), with nonnegative entries summing to one within atol (|Σ μ - 1| <= atol, as InfluenceDiagrams' DecisionContext requires; it is never renormalised), or a FiniteKernel state I → dom(r). It is the joint law of the hidden state and the remembered information at the boundary; no independence is assumed. It may be left out only when f has no inputs. It may also be a RewardKernel state I → dom(r), which carries evidence seen upstream (boundary_state).
  • continuation is a real (signed) vector over the joint output states, in joint_states order of codom(r): the value the rest of the problem earns from each boundary state. It may also be a RewardKernel effect codom(r) → I, such as the continuation_effect of a suffix or the reward kernel of a closed suffix, which also carries the probability of evidence downstream. It may be left out only when f has no outputs. A zero continuation scores f's own reward only.

With evidence (ADR 0021) anywhere, in f (the evidence keyword of the diagram forms), in a state prior or in an effect continuation, the result is the evidence-weighted value E[U · 1(evidence)], with the state (ν, ρ) and the effect (m, c):

Σ_x (ρ(x) m'(x) + ν(x) c'(x)),   m' = Kᵀ m,   c' = Wᵀ m + Kᵀ c,

which composes linearly; conditional_value divides it by the evidence_mass Σ_x ν(x) m'(x) to give the expected utility given the evidence. Without evidence the mass is one and the two agree.

A wrong length, a non-finite entry, a negative or unnormalised prior, a state of mass above one or with a probability entry below -atol, or a context left out for a nonempty interface raises ArgumentError. On a closed composite, with both left out, the value is the expected utility.

For a serial composite the contextual identity holds (the bundle's INF-FIXED-CONTEXT, checked numerically by the test suite):

contextual_value(f ⋅ g; prior = μ, continuation = c)
  == contextual_value(f; prior = μ, continuation = continuation_value(g; continuation = c))
  == contextual_value(f; prior = μ, continuation = 0)
     + contextual_value(g; prior = boundary_law(f; prior = μ), continuation = c)

With evidence in g the first equation needs the effect, which carries g's evidence mass: contextual_value(f; prior = μ, continuation = continuation_effect(g; continuation = c)); and the second the state, which carries f's: contextual_value(g; prior = boundary_state(f; prior = μ), continuation = c).

CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams.export_reward_certificate — Method
export_reward_certificate(o::OpenInfluenceDiagram, kernels, utilities, σ::Strategy;
                          numeric_mode = :binary64_exact, exact_tables = nothing,
                          description = "", max_entries = 1_000_000)
    -> OrderedDict{String,Any}
export_reward_certificate(o::OpenInfluenceDiagram, m::InfluenceDiagramModel,
                          σ::Strategy = strategy(m); kwargs...)

The OpenInfluence.RewardCertificate/v1 record of the open influence diagram o under the deterministic strategy σ (ADR 0019): its structure, observability flags, chance and utility tables, the strategy, and the reward kernel (K, W) that interpret computes for them. The Lean checker in proofs/, lake exe check_certificate, reads it. The arguments are those of interpret, dictionary and model forms alike (a model with evidence raises ArgumentError).

The result is JSON-compatible data: an OrderedDict (OrderedCollections' type, the one BayesianNetworks and InfluenceDiagrams return for their documents) whose keys, and the fields of every named tuple inside it, are in the format's order, so JSON3.write emits the same bytes on every Julia version. Its keys are format, numeric_mode, metadata (producer, producer version, Julia version and description), network, observability, chance_tables, utilities, strategy and result:

  • network is the body of CategoricalBayesianNetworks' OpenNet.RawCertificate/v1 (open_network_certificate): dense 0-based variable indices in part order, ranks (the 0-based positions in a topological order of the causal-plus-information graph), state and input rows with their 1-based positions, and attributed boundary rows. The chance mechanisms come first, then each decision as a mechanism record whose reference is policy(name) and whose input rows are its information inputs.
  • observability holds one flag per boundary row.
  • Every table is a flat list of cells in lexicographic order of its axes, the last axis fastest, each axis over its variable's states in position order. Chance tables have the parents in input-position order, then the child; utilities their scope in position order; each distinct decision name has one policy, over its first decision's information inputs, whose cells are 1-based action state positions; K and W have the input rows, then the output rows. They are converted from the outputs-first, first-variable-fastest layout of the RewardKernel.

With numeric_mode = :binary64_exact a number cell is (f64 = word), the 16 lowercase hexadecimal digits of the cell's IEEE binary64 bit word, as in InfluenceDiagrams' export_dve_certificate. With numeric_mode = :rational_exact every chance and utility cell also carries an exact companion (q = (num, den), f64 = word), taken from exact_tables, a dictionary keyed by (:chance, mechanism_part_id, coordinates) and (:utility, utility_part_id, coordinates), with zero-based coordinate tuples in the table's axis order, to exact integers or rationals. It must cover every chance and utility cell and nothing else, and each companion must round, to nearest with ties to even, to its Float64 cell. The result cells stay binary64 words. No rational is inferred from a Float64, and no table is renormalised.

Errors:

  • invalid keyword values (an unknown numeric_mode, exact_tables in binary64 mode or missing in rational mode, a malformed companion key or value, a description that is not a string, a negative max_entries) raise ArgumentError;
  • o is validated first and keeps the errors of validate; the tables and the strategy keep those of interpret (among them MissingKernelError, KernelBindingError, MissingUtilityError, UtilityScopeError, IncompleteStrategyError, PolicySignatureError, ModelTooLargeError), and a StochasticPolicy raises ArgumentError, since the format records deterministic policies;
  • what the format cannot record raises RewardCertificateError with a code: :unset_reference, :unsupported_reference, :policy_reference_on_chance, :inconsistent_decision, :too_many_entries (more than max_entries cells in all, counted from the structure before any table is read or interpret runs), :non_finite, :missing_companion, :extra_companion and :companion_rounding.

The certificate records the diagram as Julia holds it, not the operations that built it; JSON parsing, this exporter, Catlab's colimits and Julia's Float64 arithmetic are trusted (ADR 0019). See the manual page "Reward certificates".

CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams.regrets — Method
regrets(components, kernels, utilities, σ::Strategy; prior = nothing,
        continuation = nothing, evidence = nothing, perfect = false,
        no_forgetting = true, max_policies = 1_000_000, max_states,
        atol) -> Vector{Float64}

For each component of the chain, how much more it could earn in its context (component_contexts) by changing its own part of σ alone: the optimum of contextual_optimize in that context minus the component's contextual_value there. Each regret is the gain in the value of the whole chain from that unilateral change, by the contextual identity, and is zero (up to rounding) for every component exactly when σ is an equilibrium. A component without decisions has regret zero.

The search for each component is contextual_optimize's, with its max_policies limit and its PolicySearchTooLargeError; the other keywords and errors are those of component_contexts.

Given evidence (evidence, or a chain context that carries it), values are conditional values (conditional_value), the expected utility given all of the evidence, and each regret is the gain in the whole chain's conditional value from the component's unilateral change. A strategy under which the evidence is impossible has no conditional value (ImpossibleEvidenceError).

With perfect = true each regret is the largest gain over the component's joint input states: for each input state x, the best response's value from x against the component's continuation, less the current strategy's value from x, maximised over x. It is zero exactly when the component's strategy is optimal from every input state, including states the chain never reaches, which rules out equilibria sustained by choices there (subgame perfection; the Lean module Perfection, threat_perfect_iff). A perfect regret is at least the ordinary one. It is meaningful when the component's decisions observe its inputs: a policy cannot depend on a hidden input, so a component with one may have no strategy optimal from every input state, and then every perfect regret it has is positive. Perfect regrets take no evidence (ArgumentError).

Catlab.WiringDiagrams.WiringDiagramAlgebras.oapply — Method
oapply(uwd::UndirectedWiringDiagram, diagrams::Vector{<:OpenInfluenceDiagram};
       validate = true, no_forgetting = true) -> OpenInfluenceDiagram
oapply(uwd::UndirectedWiringDiagram, diagrams::Dict{Symbol,<:OpenInfluenceDiagram}; kw...)

Compose open influence diagrams along an undirected wiring diagram (for example one written with @relation). Box i receives diagrams[i] (or, for the dictionary form, the diagram named like the box); its ports are the interface variables of the diagram, inputs first, then outputs. Diagrams whose ports meet at a junction must agree there in variable, states and reference (BayesianNetworks' InterfaceMismatchError, naming the junction) and in observability (ObservabilityMismatchError). An outer port on a junction that no box touches is refused with InterfaceMismatchError(:missing_junction, ...). A box count that differs from the number of diagrams raises ArgumentError.

Each junction carries one flag, and each port the flag of its variable on its foot. A pass-through variable, one on both feet of a diagram, has two ports, an input port and an output port, whose flags differ when the diagram flags the variable differently on its two feet (see Open). Wired to the same junction, the two ports must agree like any two ports there, so different flags raise ObservabilityMismatchError. Wired to two junctions, each junction keeps its own flag; the colimit joins them into one variable, since both ports name it, and an outer port on either junction carries that junction's flag. Whether a decision may read the variable is then decided on the composite by rule 3, and rule 6 is checked as for compose.

The apex is the colimit computed by Catlab's oapply for structured multicospans. The inputs of the result are the outer-port variables without a generator (a mechanism or a decision) and its outputs the others, in outer-port order, each with its junction's flag. Undirected diagrams cannot express the typed-interface rule, so with validate = true each diagram is validated first and the composite afterwards, as for compose but without demanding unique names, rule 6 included unless no_forgetting = false. A junction that receives two generated variables gives BayesianNetworks' DuplicateGeneratorError or InfluenceDiagrams' DecisionUniquenessError, and a cycle CyclicBayesNetError or, through an information arc, InfluenceDiagrams' InvalidInformationSetError.

InfluenceDiagrams.optimize — Method
optimize(o::OpenInfluenceDiagram, kernels, utilities; evidence = nothing,
         backend = DecisionVariableElimination(), atol = DEFAULT_ATOL, kwargs...) -> DecisionSolution
optimize(o::OpenInfluenceDiagram, m::InfluenceDiagramModel; kwargs...)

Solve a closed composite (ADR 0018, decision 4): o must have no inputs and no outputs, as compose(f, g) of an f : I → Y and a g : Y → I has. Its apex is instantiated with the tables of kernels and utilities (keyed as for interpret: by KernelRef or target variable name, and by utility name or utility_ref), and InfluenceDiagrams' optimize solves it with backend, a DecisionBackend (DecisionVariableElimination or ExhaustivePolicySearch). The result is that function's DecisionSolution: the maximal expected utility and a deterministic strategy for every decision of the composite. The keywords are passed on (atol, max_states).

An open o raises ArgumentError: a component with a boundary has no expected utility of its own until a prior and a continuation are given, which is what contextual_optimize takes. There is deliberately no operation that optimises the components of a composite one by one and composes the results; that is not sound (the bundle's INF-ISOLATED-OBSTRUCTION).

evidence (ADR 0021) is hard evidence on the composite's variables (likelihood evidence raises ArgumentError: an InfluenceDiagrams model observes states only, and contextual_optimize takes it), checked as for interpret and recorded on the model with observe; the solver then maximises the expected utility given the evidence, with InfluenceDiagrams' rules (decision variable elimination needs evidence without an action among its causal ancestors; ExhaustivePolicySearch takes any, skipping strategies under which it is impossible). The model form reads kernels(m), bound_utilities(m) and the model's evidence, joined with the keyword's. o is validated first. A missing table raises MissingKernelError or MissingUtilityError; the solver's own errors pass through, among them IrregularDiagramError from decision variable elimination on a composite without no-forgetting (built with no_forgetting = false; ExhaustivePolicySearch solves those) and PolicySearchTooLargeError.
