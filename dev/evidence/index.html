Evidence across a boundary

An observation made in one component of a composite can change what is believed in every other: a sighting downstream is evidence about the habitat upstream, and a survey reading upstream changes the value of a decision downstream. ADR 0021 says how evidence crosses a boundary between open influence diagrams, and what has to be carried with it.

A component with evidence

interpret takes hard evidence on any variable of a component's apex, its inputs and outputs included:

r = interpret(o, kernels, utilities, σ; evidence = [:Sighting => :seen])

Evidence may also be soft: a likelihood vector, :Weather => [0.2, 0.9], one entry in [0, 1] per state, the probability of an observation given each state. It acts as the hard observation of a virtual child variable with that likelihood would.

The result is still a RewardKernel (K, W), but K(y | x) is now the probability of reaching the output state y and seeing the evidence, and W the reward earned on those paths. K is substochastic: its column sums are the probability of the evidence given each input state. Nothing is normalised. Reward kernels with evidence compose exactly as others do, and the evidence of a composite is the union of its components' evidence; evidence on a variable of the shared boundary may be given to either side or to both, since an indicator is idempotent.

Why the mass must cross the boundary

Normalising each component on its own evidence before composing is not sound. Take a uniform binary state worth 1 when it is true, and a sighting nine times likelier when the state is true. Given the sighting the state is true with probability 9/10, and that is the value. Normalised on its own, the component holding the sighting has the evidence with probability one from either state, and composing it with the prior gives 1/2: the likelihood of the evidence as a function of the boundary state is exactly what normalising discards. The Lean module Evidence proves this witness (local_normalisation_obstruction), and the test suite reproduces it.

States, effects and the conditional value

The contexts of ADR 0018 and the lenses of ADR 0020 assume that what follows a component has total probability one. With evidence they carry the mass:

  • a state on a component's inputs, a reward kernel I → X, pairs the unnormalised law ν(x) of reaching x with the evidence seen so far and the reward-weighted law ρ(x) earned so far; boundary_state pushes it forward through a component;
  • an effect on a component's outputs, a reward kernel Y → I, pairs the probability m(y) of the evidence still to come and the evidence-weighted value c(y) of what follows; continuation_effect pulls it back through a component.

A plain prior is the state (μ, 0) and a plain continuation the effect (1, c), so every function that takes a prior or a continuation also takes a state or an effect. Against a state and an effect, a component has

  • an evidence_mass Σ_x ν(x) m'(x),
  • an evidence-weighted value, contextual_value, Σ_x (ρ(x) m'(x) + ν(x) c'(x)),
  • and a conditional_value, their ratio: the expected utility given all of the evidence,

where (m', c') is the effect pulled back through the component. Both pairings are the same at every cut of a chain (the Lean theorems mass_push and value_push), so the conditional value of a composite can be computed at any boundary from the two contexts there:

s = boundary_state(prefix, kernels, utilities, σ)                     # forward
e = continuation_effect(suffix, kernels, utilities, τ;
                        evidence = [:Sighting => :seen])              # backward
conditional_value(middle, kernels, utilities, ρ; prior = s, continuation = e)
# == conditional_value(prefix ⋅ middle ⋅ suffix, kernels, utilities, σ ∪ ρ ∪ τ;
#                      evidence = [:Sighting => :seen])

Without evidence the mass is one, the state's probability row is boundary_law, the effect's reward row is continuation_value, and the conditional value is the contextual value.

Optimisation and equilibria given evidence

contextual_optimize takes evidence, and a state or an effect as its context, and then maximises the conditional value over the legal strategies, from value tables built in one pass over the component's joint states, skipping strategies under which the evidence is impossible, as InfluenceDiagrams' exhaustive search does on a closed model with evidence. On a closed composite, optimize(o, kernels, utilities; evidence) records the evidence on the model and calls InfluenceDiagrams' solvers. When the evidence lies downstream of a decision, its probability depends on the strategy, and the conditional value is not the evidence-weighted value up to a constant.

The chain functions of Lenses and equilibria, component_contexts, regrets, is_equilibrium and best_response_dynamics, take evidence on the variables of the chain. The contexts are then states and effects, each component's conditional value in its context is the whole chain's, and every regret is the gain in the chain's conditional value from one component's change alone.

Rare and impossible evidence

conditional_value follows ADR 0014. A binary64 run is trusted when its evidence mass is finite and at least floatmin(Float64) and none of its products of nonzero operands fell below floatmin. Otherwise the value is recomputed exactly in Rational{BigInt} and rounded correctly, so a mass that only underflowed gives the right answer; only a mass that is exactly zero raises ImpossibleEvidenceError. The diagram forms recompute from the model's tables; the reward-kernel form has only its tables, and decides on them. A model with tolerated negative entries raises IndeterminatePosteriorError when the mass is within the tolerance budget of zero. evidence_mass is an unnormalised quantity and is returned as computed.

What is proved

The Lean module Evidence proves, over arbitrary real tables, that the actions on states and effects are functorial with no normalisation hypothesis, that the evidence mass and the evidence-weighted value are the same on either side of a reward kernel, that the contextual identity holds without normalisation once the mass is carried, that hard evidence on a boundary is idempotent and commutes, and the local-normalisation witness. For the syntax, it proves that weighting the interpretation by a likelihood on the generators' values is functorial under serial composition and tensor. The binary64 rules above are not proved; the test suite checks the Julia functions against InfluenceDiagrams' expected_utility of the closed composite with the evidence observed.
