Reward certificates

export_reward_certificate writes an open influence diagram, a deterministic strategy for it and the reward kernel (K, W) that interpret computed for that strategy as an OpenInfluence.RewardCertificate/v1 record (ADR 0019). The Lean project in proofs/ checks such a record with lake exe check_certificate. The record ties the numbers Julia computed for one concrete diagram to the definition of the semantics the Lean theorems are stated for, cell by cell, with the error stated.

using CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams

m = umbrella_model()
chance = Open(influence_diagram(:Weather => [:sunny, :rainy],
                                :Forecast => [:sunny, :cloudy, :rainy];
                                mechanisms=[:Weather => (), :Forecast => :Weather],
                                closed=false);
              outputs=[:Weather, :Forecast], observed=[:Forecast])
decide = Open(influence_diagram(:Weather => [:sunny, :rainy],
                                :Forecast => [:sunny, :cloudy, :rainy],
                                :Umbrella => [:take, :leave];
                                decisions=[:Umbrella => :Forecast],
                                utilities=[:U => (:Weather, :Umbrella)], closed=false);
              inputs=[:Weather, :Forecast], observed=[:Forecast])
σ = Strategy(:Umbrella => deterministic_policy(m, :Umbrella, [:leave, :leave, :take]))
kernels = Dict{Symbol,FiniteKernel}(x => kernel(m, x) for x in chance_names(syntax(m)))

certificate = export_reward_certificate(decide, kernels, bound_utilities(m), σ)
closed = export_reward_certificate(compose(chance, decide), kernels, bound_utilities(m), σ)

The arguments are those of interpret. The model form export_reward_certificate(o, m, σ = strategy(m)) reads kernels(m) and bound_utilities(m) through the apex's references, and refuses a model with evidence.

The result is JSON-compatible data, an OrderedDict{String,Any} whose keys, and the fields of every named tuple inside it, are in the format's order. JSON3, or another writer that keeps insertion order, therefore writes the same bytes on every Julia version:

using JSON3
write("umbrella-decision.json", JSON3.write(certificate) * "\n")

What a certificate records

KeyContent
format"OpenInfluence.RewardCertificate/v1"
numeric_mode"binary64_exact" or "rational_exact"
metadataproducer, producer_version, julia_version and the caller's description
networkthe structure: variables, generators and boundary rows
observabilityone flag, "observed" or "hidden", per input row and per output row
chance_tablesone table per chance mechanism
utilitiesone record per utility: its name, positioned scope rows and table
strategyone policy per distinct decision name
result{"arithmetic": "binary64", "K": [...], "W": [...]}

The JSON Schema (draft 2020-12) gives the shape of each field. Its property order is the key order of the certificate. Cross-field conditions are for the checker: cell counts, positions, ranks, flag counts, information locality, row sums and the result.

The network

network has exactly the keys of the body of CategoricalBayesianNetworks' OpenNet.RawCertificate/v1 (open_network_certificate): variableCount, variableData, mechanisms, inputs and outputs, with the same record shapes and reference tags (noRef, named, pointMass and policy).

  • Variable indices are dense and 0-based, in the apex's Variable part order. Positions (of states, inputs and scope) are the actual 1-based *_position attributes, and rows are written in part order with those positions, not sorted.
  • Each variable's rank is its 0-based position in a topological order of the causal-plus-information graph, so ranks increase along every generator's input rows.
  • The generators are the chance mechanisms, in Mechanism part order, then the decisions, in Decision part order. A decision is a mechanism record whose name is its decision name, whose kernelRef is {"tag": "policy", "decision": name}, whose target is its action variable and whose inputRows are its information inputs, each with its information_position. Generator indices are 0-based positions in this list.
  • Boundary rows hold the apex variable each foot variable maps to and the attributes read from the foot itself. A copied output is two rows on one variable, and a pass-through variable is a row on each foot.

Decision precedence rows are not recorded, and neither is no-forgetting: the semantics for a fixed strategy reads neither.

Cell layout

Every table is a flat list of cells in lexicographic order of its axes, the last axis varying fastest, each axis running over its variable's states in position order. A table with no axes has one cell. The axes are:

  • a chance table: the parents in input-position order, then the child. A repeated parent is a repeated axis. The record is {"mechanism": g, "cells": [...]}, with g the generator index.
  • a utility: its scope rows in position order, a repeated variable being a repeated axis.
  • a policy: the information inputs, in position order, of the first decision generator with that name. Each cell is the 1-based state position of the chosen action. A ConstantPolicy is written as its table. Equally named decisions share one policy (ADR 0018), so they must agree in information axis lengths and action state count.
  • K and W: the input rows, in row order, then the output rows, in row order.

A RewardKernel holds its tables outputs first, with joint states enumerated first variable fastest (FiniteKernels' joint_states). The exporter converts them: the certificate cell at input state x and output state y is kernel(r).table[y..., x...] and reward(r)[y..., x...]. In the decision half of the umbrella above, the inputs are (Weather, Forecast) and there are no outputs, so W lists the expected utility for (sunny, sunny), (sunny, cloudy), (sunny, rainy), (rainy, sunny), and so on: 100, 100, 20, 0, 0, 70.

Numeric modes

A number cell is an InfluenceDiagrams certificate value (as export_dve_certificate writes it), so the Lean side reuses that project's value decoder:

  • {"f64": word}: word is 16 lowercase hexadecimal digits, the IEEE binary64 bit pattern. Its exact value is the dyadic rational the bits denote. Non-finite values are never written.
  • {"q": {"num": "...", "den": "..."}, "f64": word}: an exact rational companion and the binary64 word, where f64 is the nearest-even rounding of q. The exact value of the cell is q.

In binary64_exact mode (the default) every cell is {"f64"}. A runtime acceptance of a kernel within atol does not make its binary64 rows sum to exactly one, and the checker makes no semantic claim when they do not. It reports the largest row deviation instead. Tables whose cells are dyadic (1/2, 1/4, 3/8, ...) are exact in binary64; the fixture dyadic.json is such a model.

In rational_exact mode every chance and utility cell is {"q", "f64"}, and the result cells stay {"f64"}. The companions come from exact_tables, a dictionary keyed like InfluenceDiagrams' companions, with zero-based coordinates in the table's axis order:

(:chance, mechanism_part_id, Tuple(zero_based_coordinates)) => exact integer or rational
(:utility, utility_part_id, Tuple(zero_based_coordinates))  => exact integer or rational

It must cover every chance and utility cell and nothing else. Each companion is checked to round, to nearest with ties to even, to the Float64 cell it accompanies, by BayesianNetworks' correctly rounded _nearest_binary64 (ADR 0016); a zero companion matches either signed zero. No rational is inferred from a Float64 and no table is renormalised. This mode is the way to certify a model such as the umbrella, whose decimal tables (0.7, 0.15, ...) are not dyadic.

Limits and errors

max_entries (default 1_000_000) bounds the total number of cells: the chance tables, utilities, policies, K and W. It is checked from the structure alone, before any table is read and before interpret runs.

Invalid keyword values raise ArgumentError: an unknown numeric_mode, exact_tables in binary64_exact mode or missing in rational_exact mode, a companion key that is not of the form above (or two keys naming one cell) or a companion that is not an exact integer or rational, a description that is not a string, and a max_entries that is not a nonnegative integer.

The diagram is validated first, and keeps the errors of validate. The tables and the strategy keep those of interpret: MissingKernelError, KernelBindingError, MissingUtilityError, UtilityScopeError, IncompleteStrategyError, PolicySignatureError, ModelTooLargeError, and ArgumentError for a StochasticPolicy.

What the format cannot record raises RewardCertificateError, whose code, path and message say what and where:

CodeCondition
:unset_referencea space_ref or kernel_ref has no value
:unsupported_referencea reference of a type the format has no tag for
:policy_reference_on_chancea chance mechanism carries a PolicyRef
:inconsistent_decisionequally named decisions of different shapes
:too_many_entriesmore than max_entries cells
:non_finitea cell that is not finite
:missing_companiona chance or utility cell without an exact companion
:extra_companiona companion that matches no cell
:companion_roundinga companion that does not round to its cell

Fixtures

scripts/write_certificate_fixtures.jl writes the fixtures the Lean checker reads into proofs/certificate-fixtures/, as compact JSON with a trailing newline:

FileContent
umbrella-decision.jsonthe decision half of the umbrella split, with Forecast observed on input
umbrella-closed.jsonthe umbrella split composed back: empty interfaces, K = [1], W = [EU]
two-stage-composite.jsonthe oil wildcatter composed from its test and drill pieces
relay.jsona pass-through variable and a copied output
dyadic.jsondyadic chance tables, so that binary64 rows sum to exactly one
umbrella-rational.jsonthe closed umbrella in rational_exact mode

The test suite builds them with the same code and compares them with the committed files byte for byte, apart from metadata.julia_version, which names the Julia that ran the exporter. Run julia --project scripts/write_certificate_fixtures.jl --check to check them, and without --check to rewrite them after a deliberate change to the format, which has to be made in the Lean checker too.

Trust boundary

A checker that accepts a certificate has checked the record, not the program that produced it (ADR 0019). These steps are trusted, not proved:

  • JSON parsing;
  • this exporter;
  • Catlab's colimits, which built the composite;
  • Julia's Float64 arithmetic in interpret.

A certificate certifies the diagram it records, not the operations that built it. A certificate of a composite shows that Julia's numbers match the Lean semantics for the composite as recorded; on its own it does not show that Julia's composite is the Lean composite of its parts. Composition certificates, below, add that, for the semantics. Contextual values and contextual optimisation are not covered by version 1.

Composition certificates

export_composition_certificate writes an OpenInfluence.CompositionCertificate/v1 record (ADR 0023): the reward certificates of two parts A and B and of compose(A, B), under one strategy. lake exe check_composition accepts it when

  1. each of the three is accepted, as check_certificate would accept it;
  2. the interfaces line up: A's output rows have the attributes of B's input rows, the composite's input rows those of A's and its output rows those of B's;
  3. every recorded cell of the composite is within the tolerance of the composite of the parts' recorded cells: K_C(x, z) of Σ_y K_A(x, y) K_B(y, z) and W_C(x, z) of Σ_y (W_A(x, y) K_B(y, z) + K_A(x, y) W_B(y, z)).

The Lean theorems composition_sound_K and composition_sound_W then bound, at every (x, z), the gap between the decoded composite's exact reward kernel and the composite of the decoded parts' exact reward kernels, by 2τ plus τ times sums of recorded cells. Since each certificate's exact tables are the interpret of its decoded diagram, this ties Julia's composite to the Lean composite of its parts, for the semantics. The syntax of the composite, its net up to isomorphism, is not checked.

cc = export_composition_certificate(chance, decide, kernels(m), bound_utilities(m), σ)

export_tensor_certificate writes an OpenInfluence.TensorCertificate/v1 record for otimes(A, B) in the same way, and lake exe check_composition reads either kind by its format string. For a tensor the composite's interfaces must be the parts' side by side, A's first, and every recorded cell must be within the tolerance of the product of the parts' cells: K_A(x, y) K_B(x', y'), and W_A K_B + K_A W_B for the reward. The theorems tensor_sound_K and tensor_sound_W bound the gap as for composition.

In :rational_exact mode exact_tables is a named tuple (left = ..., right = ..., composite = ...), each keyed by its own diagram's part ids. The fixtures in proofs/composition-fixtures/ (written by scripts/write_certificate_fixtures.jl, checked by make check-compositions) hold two accepted compositions and three rejected at the part, alignment and composition stages.

Colimit certificates

A composition certificate checks numbers; the structure of the composite, that it is the colimit Catlab says it is, stays trusted. export_colimit_certificate writes an OpenInfluence.ColimitCertificate/v1 record (ADR 0025) that also records the structure: the reward certificates of the parts and of the composite, the operation's name, and Catlab's cocone maps as legs, one per part, giving the composite's index of each of the part's variables, generators and utilities. It covers every operation:

export_colimit_certificate(:compose, A, B, kernels, utilities, σ)
export_colimit_certificate(:otimes, A, B, kernels, utilities, σ)
export_colimit_certificate(:glue, A, B, kernels, utilities, σ; along = [:x => :x])
export_colimit_certificate(uwd, [A, B, C], kernels, utilities, σ)   # oapply

lake exe check_composition reads it by its format string and accepts when every reward certificate is accepted and the legs are a colimit witness:

  • the parts' generators, and their utilities, land on the composite's one to one;
  • variables keep their attributes; generators their labels, targets, ordered parents, chance tables and recorded policies; utilities their ordered scopes and tables;
  • two distinct part variables are joined only when both are boundary variables of their parts;
  • every output of the composite is one of its inputs or a part's boundary variable.

The Lean theorems colimit_sound_K and colimit_sound_W then give the composite's exact semantics, with no tolerance, as the junction sum of the parts': K_C(x, y) is the sum, over boundary coordinates of the parts that agree wherever their variables are joined and with x and y, of the product of the parts' K, and W_C(x, y) the corresponding sum of W_i Π_{j≠i} K_j. For compose this is the composite of reward kernels and for otimes the tensor. The witness is checked on the decoded records, not as an isomorphism of the Lean syntax; the Lean module SyntaxCategory proves separately that the semantics respects isomorphism of diagrams. For glue the second part is recorded as glued, with its glued inputs renamed after their partners.

In :rational_exact mode exact_tables is a named tuple (parts = [...], composite = ...), one companion table per part. The colimit-* fixtures of proofs/composition-fixtures/ (written by colimit_fixtures) hold four accepted certificates, one per operation, and three rejected: a leg sending a generator to the wrong place, a composite whose recorded policy is not its part's, and binary64.
