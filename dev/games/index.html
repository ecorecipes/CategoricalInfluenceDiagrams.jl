Lenses and equilibria

Optimisation of open influence diagrams is contextual (ADR 0018): a component f : X → Y is valued against a prior μ on its inputs and a continuation c on its outputs, and the optimum of a composite is not the composite of the components' optima. ADR 0020 adds the solution concept that does compose, equilibrium in the sense of open games, and the lenses that carry the contexts it needs.

The lens of a component

Under a fixed strategy a component is a reward kernel (K, W). It transports a context in two directions:

  • forward, a prior μ on its inputs becomes the law ν = μ K on its outputs (boundary_law);
  • backward, a continuation c on its outputs becomes the value function x ↦ Σ_y (W(y, x) + K(y | x) c(y)) on its inputs (continuation_value).

The pair is a lens. For a chain f₁ ⋅ f₂ ⋅ … ⋅ fₙ under a strategy σ, the forward pass gives each component the law on its inputs and the backward pass the value of its outputs. component_contexts returns these contexts (μᵢ, cᵢ). By the contextual identity, component i's value in its context is the value of the whole chain less the reward of the components before it, which i's own strategy cannot change. The Lean module Lens proves that the lens of a serial composite is the composite of the lenses (lens_comp), so the contexts do not depend on how the chain is bracketed.

Equilibria

A strategy of the chain is an equilibrium when every component's part of it is a best response in that component's context: no component can raise the value of the whole chain by changing its own strategy alone. regrets returns, for each component, what it would gain by its best response (contextual_optimize in its context); is_equilibrium checks that every regret is at most a tolerance.

chain = [early, late]                 # e.g. the components of the third vignette
σ = merge_strategies(Strategy(:A => ConstantPolicy(:no)), Strategy(:B => ConstantPolicy(:no)))
component_contexts(chain, kernels, utilities, σ)   # [(prior, continuation), ...]
regrets(chain, kernels, utilities, σ)              # [3.0, 0.0]: the early component gains 3
is_equilibrium(chain, kernels, utilities, σ)       # false

best_response_dynamics replaces one component's strategy at a time by a best response in its current context, while that strictly improves it, and stops at an equilibrium. The value of the chain never decreases.

What an equilibrium is not

An equilibrium is a person-by-person optimum: each component is optimal given the others. The global optimum of the closed composite is always an equilibrium (seq_sound), but an equilibrium can be worth less. Two decisions that must agree, where the later does not observe the earlier, have an equilibrium in which neither acts, worth 1, and one in which both act, worth 2 (coordination_equilibrium_not_optimal). Best-response dynamics started from "neither acts" stays there. This is the local optimality of single policy updating in limited-memory influence diagrams (Lauritzen and Nilsson, 2001), at the level of components.

So:

  • use optimize on the closed composite for the optimum;
  • use component_contexts to see what each component is optimised against, and regrets to check whether a proposed strategy can be improved by any one component on its own;
  • use best_response_dynamics when components are owned or revised separately and the question is where separate revision settles, not what is best overall.

In the third vignette's counterexample, where components optimised in isolation compose to 1 against an optimum of 4, the equilibria are exactly the strategies that take the early action, and they attain 4: optimising each component against the context its neighbours induce repairs that example, though not every example.

Limited-memory chains, where the later decisions do not remember the earlier ones, are composed with no_forgetting = false, a keyword all four functions accept and pass to compose. Behavioural strategies are covered by ADR 0022: a chain may start from one, and best responses are deterministic. The tensor of games is proved in Lean (exact for product priors, not expressible through one factor's context for correlated ones); the Julia chain functions are serial.

Perfect equilibria

An equilibrium can rest on a choice at a state it never reaches. A later component's policy there is a best response whatever it is, because the state has probability zero, and it can still deter an earlier component. regrets(...; perfect = true) and is_equilibrium(...; perfect = true) ask more: every component optimal from every one of its input states, against its continuation. This is subgame perfection, found by backward induction. The Lean module Perfection proves that perfection refines equilibrium, and gives the threat witness: not acting against an untested refusal is an equilibrium worth 1, while the perfect equilibria act and cooperate, worth the optimum 4. Perfection is meaningful when a component's decisions observe its inputs.

Shared policies

Several components may have to use one policy: a rule applied every year, or decisions that cannot be told apart, as in the absent-minded driver. compose refuses two decisions of one name, so sharing is declared: shared = [[:D1, :D2]] ties D2 to the policy of D1, which the strategy gives (position by position; the information states and actions must agree in size). The three functions of ADR 0024 take it.

The optimum of a shared policy can be randomised. In the absent-minded driver, continuing with probability 2/3 at both junctions is worth 4/3, and each deterministic policy is worth at most 1. At that optimum each junction on its own would still change: continuing for sure at the first junction alone gains 2/3. So the equilibria above, where each component best responds on its own, are the wrong notion. What holds at an optimum is the first-order condition of Piccione and Rubinstein's multi-self consistency. Change the policy towards another one, value each use's change alone in its own context, and the gains sum to at most zero. The Lean module SharedPolicy proves it (shared_optimum_first_order), from a product rule for serial composites, and gives the driver's per-use gains 2/3 and -2/3.

  • policy_regrets returns, for every policy, shared or not, the largest sum of per-use gains over deterministic replacements: the first-order gain. For a policy used once it is exactly the gain of changing that decision alone.
  • is_policy_equilibrium tests that every such regret is at most tol.
  • Given evidence (or a chain context that carries it), the value is the chain's conditional value V = N / Z, and a use's gain is the first-order change of the ratio, (N_u - V Z_u) / Z (first_order_conditional in Lean).
  • shared_policy_search searches behavioural strategies whose probabilities are multiples of 1 / grid, valuing each on the composite; the grid contains the deterministic strategies and, for a suitable grid, the randomised optimum.
s = shared_policy_search(chain, kernels, utilities; shared = [[:X1, :X2]], grid = 3)
policy_regrets(chain, kernels, utilities, s.strategy; shared = [[:X1, :X2]])

The first-order condition is necessary at an optimum, not sufficient. When every decision has its own policy, deterministic strategies suffice (Kuhn's theorem, proved in the Lean module Kuhn for open influence diagrams), and optimize on the closed composite finds the optimum.
