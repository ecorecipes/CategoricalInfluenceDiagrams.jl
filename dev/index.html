CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams.jl

Open influence diagrams as structured cospans, following ADR 0018 of the ecosystem. The package is the decision-layer analogue of CategoricalBayesianNetworks.jl: it gives the influence diagrams of InfluenceDiagrams.jl open boundaries and composes them, keeping Catlab out of the model layer (ADR 0009).

What is here

  • Open influence diagrams. An OpenInfluenceDiagram is a Catlab structured cospan whose apex is an InfluenceDiagram and whose feet are VariableSpaces, the same feet as those of an open Bayesian network. Build one with Open.

  • Observability. Every interface variable is :observed, remembered information that a later decision may read, or :hidden. The flags sit beside the cospan, one per interface position, rather than in the feet, so that the feet stay interchangeable with those of CategoricalBayesianNetworks.jl. Everything is hidden unless declared observed, by name on every foot (observed = [...]) or foot by foot (observed = (inputs = [...], outputs = [...])). A pass-through variable observed on the input foot and hidden on the output foot is forgotten at the boundary; hidden on the input foot and observed on the output foot, it is revealed by the component, whose own decisions still may not read it. Two interfaces that agree on variables and states but not on flags do not match (ObservabilityMismatchError).

  • The typed-interface rule, checked by validation_errors and on construction:

    1. every variable has at most one generator, a chance mechanism or a decision;
    2. an input-foot variable has no generator in the apex;
    3. a decision reads only internal variables and observed input-foot variables;
    4. a utility scope may read any apex variable, inputs included;
    5. the causal-plus-information graph is acyclic;
    6. composition preserves no-forgetting when the components have it: checked on every composite, reported as a NoForgettingError naming the decision and the missing information arc.

  • Composition. compose, glue, otimes and oapply are pushouts or coproducts of ACSets, with the rule checked again on the composite. Decisions and utilities stay inside their components; only variables cross a boundary.

  • Open Bayesian networks embed with every interface variable hidden: OpenInfluenceDiagram(o::OpenBayesNet), inverted by OpenBayesNet(d).

  • Semantics for a fixed strategy. A RewardKernel (K, W) pairs a FiniteKernel K from the input interface to the output interface with a table W of the same shape: the probability-weighted reward earned on the way. Reward kernels compose by (K₁, W₁) ; (K₂, W₂) = (K₁K₂, W₁K₂ + K₁W₂) and tensor by (K₁ ⊗ K₂, W₁ ⊗ K₂ + K₁ ⊗ W₂), with identities, copies, discards and swaps lifted from FiniteKernels.jl with zero reward (compose, otimes, identity_reward_kernel, copy_reward_kernel, discard_reward_kernel, swap_reward_kernel). Discard is not natural: dropping an output keeps the reward earned on the way to it.

  • Interpretation. interpret maps an open influence diagram and a deterministic strategy to its reward kernel, in the layout CategoricalBayesianNetworks.jl uses for open networks. The strategy of a composite is the disjoint union of its components' strategies (merge_strategies). On a closed composite the result is (1, EU), with EU the expected utility that InfluenceDiagrams.jl computes, and the test suite checks interpret against that oracle under compose, glue, otimes and oapply, on every strategy of the umbrella and oil-wildcatter splits, on the SPEC §46 grazing diagram at two boundaries, and on random small diagrams.

  • Optimisation, contextual and never isolated. A composite is an ordinary InfluenceDiagram, and optimize hands a closed composite to the solvers of InfluenceDiagrams.jl. An open component f : X → Y has no expected utility of its own: contextual_value scores it under a fixed strategy against an explicit prior μ on its joint input states and an explicit continuation c on its joint output states, Σ_x μ(x) Σ_y (W(y, x) + K(y | x) c(y)), and contextual_optimize maximises that over the legal deterministic strategies, by handing the component to the ExhaustivePolicySearch of InfluenceDiagrams.jl with the prior over its input variables and the continuation over its output variables as a DecisionContext (its brute-force path, through interpret, is the cross-check). For a serial composite f ⋅ g the contexts are the suffix's value function (continuation_value) and the prefix's boundary law (boundary_law), and the contextual identity EU_{f;g}(σ, τ; μ, c) = EU_f(σ; μ, c_τ) = L_σ + EU_g(τ; ν_σ, c) holds; the test suite checks it against the closed-composite oracle, and checks that both orders of contextual maximisation reach the optimum the solvers find. There is deliberately no "optimise each component, then compose" operation. The test suite reproduces the counterexample of the research bundle's Lean track: a fair hidden bit, an early action with utility -[A], a late action that observes only A with utilities 4[A] and 2[B = H]. Each component optimised in isolation (zero continuation, actual induced prior) composes to the value 1, while the composite attains 4. Maximising separately at each hidden boundary state would need a policy that reads the hidden input, which rule 3 rejects.

  • Lenses and equilibria (ADR 0020). Along a chain of components, component_contexts gives each component the prior and continuation the rest of the chain induces, regrets and is_equilibrium test whether any one component could gain alone, and best_response_dynamics revises one component at a time until none can. An equilibrium can be worth less than the optimum; see Lenses and equilibria.

  • Evidence across a boundary (ADR 0021). interpret takes hard evidence, which makes a component's reward kernel substochastic; boundary_state and continuation_effect carry the evidence mass across a boundary, and evidence_mass and conditional_value, the expected utility given the evidence, are the same at every cut. Normalising each component on its own evidence is not sound; see Evidence across a boundary.

  • Behavioural strategies (ADR 0022). A StochasticPolicy draws a decision's action from a table given its information, and every function that takes a strategy takes one. Searches stay over deterministic strategies, which suffice when every decision has its own policy (with or without evidence); decisions that share a name share a policy, and then randomising can help, as in the absent-minded driver, so contextual_optimize refuses shared names.

  • Reward certificates (ADR 0019). export_reward_certificate writes a diagram, its flags and tables, a deterministic strategy and the reward kernel interpret computed for it as an OpenInfluence.RewardCertificate/v1 record, for the Lean checker in proofs/ (lake exe check_certificate). See Reward certificates.

Proofs, and what is not here

The Lean project in proofs/ builds on the proof projects of CategoricalBayesianNetworks.jl and InfluenceDiagrams.jl. It proves the reward-kernel category and its symmetric monoidal structure, the functoriality of the fixed-strategy interpretation under serial composition and tensor, the contextual identity, the two obstructions of the research bundle, lenses and open-game equilibria (ADR 0020), the composition of evidence (ADR 0021), and behavioural strategies with the value of randomising (ADR 0022). Its checker for reward certificates ties Julia's interpret of a recorded diagram to that interpretation, cell by cell (ADR 0019). proofs/README.md lists the theorems and their restrictions; the test suite checks the same laws numerically.

Behavioural (randomised) policies are interpreted like deterministic ones (ADR 0022); the search for an optimum stays over deterministic strategies, which suffice when every decision has its own policy. A reward certificate checks the diagram it records, not the operation that built it; a composition certificate (ADR 0023) checks that a composite's semantics is the composite of its parts', which ties Julia's composite to the Lean composite for the semantics, though not for the syntax.
